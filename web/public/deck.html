The problem

A 16% dip can wipe you out.

Borrowing against volatile tokenized stocks is dangerous. One bad day pushes a healthy loan below its liquidation line — and your collateral is seized at the worst possible price. Most lending protocols just liquidate you and move on.

What Ward does

An autopilot that keeps your loan alive.

1

Deposit

Put up a tokenized stock (e.g. TSLA) as collateral.

2

Borrow

Draw USDG against it.

3

Buffer

Set aside a small USDG safety buffer + a trigger/target.

4

Protected

A keeper repays from your buffer before liquidation can hit.

Differentiator 1

A volatility-aware risk engine, in Stylus.

The liquidation threshold isn't fixed — it reads realized volatility from on-chain price history and adapts. Tighter when markets turn violent, looser when they calm. Rate-limited, so it never over-reacts.
Written in Rust, compiled to WASM with Arbitrum Stylus — an order of magnitude cheaper than Solidity. Cheap enough to run on every position, verifiably on-chain.
Differentiator 2

An agent you don't have to trust.

An autonomous keeper watches your health factor every block and acts before liquidation.
Its power is deliberately tiny: it can only call protect() — repay your debt from your own buffer.
It can never re-borrow, swap, move funds, or open positions. Invariant enforced by the contract and covered by tests.

Why Robinhood Chain

Credit belongs next to the stocks.

Robinhood Chain is where tokenized stocks live and where a consumer audience already is. Stock-backed credit should sit right next to the stocks themselves — same chain, same assets (we use the real TSLA token as collateral), same users.
